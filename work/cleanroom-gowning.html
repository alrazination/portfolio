Cleanroom gowning

In a semiconductor fab, a single hair can ruin a wafer. Gowning has to go top to bottom, and this simulation turns contamination into something you can see and measure.

Where it started

Contamination is invisible. New technicians can be told that hair and skin particles fall downward, but they never see it happen, so "top to bottom" feels like an arbitrary rule.

The gowning order exists to stop particles falling onto clean garments, and to keep street shoes out of the clean zone. If the simulation could show the particles, the rule would explain itself.

The core mechanic

A particle counter sits above the technician at all times, the way a real fab monitors its air. Every choice moves the number.

→

Gown top to bottom
→
The counter drops to 0 and the technician may enter the fab.

Designing the mistakes

Design decisions

A number people already trust

The particle counter mirrors real monitoring equipment, so the feedback feels like the job, not a quiz.

Two kinds of failure

Contaminating the suit and breaching the clean line are different mistakes with different causes, so they look different.

Warnings before failures

Small ordering slips get a coaching message. Only mistakes that would contaminate the fab end the attempt.

The checklist fills in

The top-down checklist ticks off as each garment goes on, reinforcing the sequence after it's been done, not before.

How it's built

SVGWeb Animations APIPointer eventsArticulate Storyline

The technician, garments, dust and footprints are all SVG, animated with the Web Animations API. Like the tire simulation, drag and drop works at any scale for embedding in Storyline, and the animation respects reduced-motion settings.
