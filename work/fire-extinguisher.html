Fight or get out?

Every workplace has extinguishers on the wall, and most people couldn’t say which one to grab. This simulation lets learners use the wrong one, safely, and watch exactly why it’s wrong.

Play it

Four fires. Pick what to grab from the wall, or get out. With the right extinguisher, pull the pin, then hold the mouse down and sweep across the base of the fire.

Where it started

Fire safety training usually ends with a poster: colour bands, fire classes, a table to memorise. Then, the one time it matters, someone grabs whatever is closest.

The rules only make sense once you know what goes wrong. Water on burning fat doesn’t just fail, it explodes. Seeing that once is worth more than reading the table ten times.

The core mechanic

Each fire is a different workplace: an office bin, an overloaded power strip, a frying pan, a storeroom. Learners choose from the extinguishers on the wall, a fire blanket, or getting out. Every wrong choice plays out.

What the learner doesWhat happens
Water or foam on a fat fire
→
Fireball. The water flashes to steam and throws burning oil across the kitchen.
Water on a burning power strip
→
An electric arc runs up the stream towards the learner.
CO₂ on burning paper
→
The flames drop, then the bin reignites.
Any extinguisher on the storeroom fire
→
It empties in seconds while the smoke drops to the floor. Getting out was the right answer.
The right extinguisher
→
Pull the pin, aim at the base and sweep. Aim at the flames and the extinguisher runs out first.

Designing the mistakes

Design decisions

Getting out is an option every time

The last fire is too big to fight. Knowing when to leave is part of the skill, so “get out” sits next to the extinguishers on every fire.

Colour bands, not labels

Extinguishers are chosen by their coloured band, as on a real wall. After four fires the colours mean something.

Technique, not just choice

Choosing correctly isn’t the end. Aim too high and the fire wins; sweep the base and it’s out in seconds.

Leaving is never wrong

Getting out of a small fire is marked safe, just not ideal, so nobody learns that leaving is a mistake.
