Wo ist der Löffel?

A 360° kitchen for A1 German. Learners look around, open cupboards and drawers, and find the word they're asked for, so the noun, its article and the object are learned together.

Audience
A1 German learners and their teachers
Topic
58 kitchen words, with articles
Core mechanic
Search a 3D room
Built with
three.js, Web Speech API

Play it

Drag to look around and click to open things. Start in Entdecken (explore) mode, then try Finden + Artikel. The game is in German, with English after each answer.

Open full screen

Built for a mouse or touchscreen at laptop size or larger. On a phone, open it full screen and turn your phone sideways.

Where it started

Beginners usually learn kitchen vocabulary from lists or flashcards: a German word next to an English one, or next to a picture. That trains matching text to text. It doesn't build the link that matters, between the word, its article and the thing in front of you.

German articles make it harder. You can't guess der, die or das from the object, so learners need to meet each noun with its article many times, in context, before it sticks.

So the question became: what if the vocabulary list were a room you could stand in?

The core mechanic

The kitchen has no labels. To find der Löffel you have to think about where a spoon lives, open the drawer, and click it. Searching makes learners predict, and every click is a small act of recall.

What the learner doesWhat happens
Click the right object
→
You hear the word with its article and a model sentence: Der Löffel ist in der Schublade.
Click the wrong object
→
You hear what you clicked, with its article. You lose a little, and learn a word anyway.
Ask for a hint
→
Three steps: a sentence saying where, the camera turns that way, then the object glows.
Choose the wrong article
→
The word comes back later in the round, with a rule that helps: Kühlschrank takes der from Schrank.

Designing the mistakes

Design decisions

Explore before you're tested

Explore mode introduces the words with no points at all. Testing comes after learners have had a chance to look around.

Opening things is free

Cupboards and drawers cost nothing to open, so nobody guesses out of fear of losing points.

Colour carries the article

Der is always blue, die red, das green. In article mode the colour stays hidden until a choice is made, so it can't give the answer away.

Mistakes come back

Words found with help, or with the wrong article, return later in the same round. It's spaced repetition without a separate review screen.

No clock, no leaderboard

Learners move on when they're ready. Beginners need time to say the word out loud.

Teacher notes built in

The start screen explains the design for teachers, so they know how to use each mode in class.
