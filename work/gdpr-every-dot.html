Where it started

Data protection training has a distance problem. It’s full of legal terms, and the people it protects never appear. “1,800 records” sounds like a file, not like 1,800 people with bank details and home addresses.

The mistakes themselves are ordinary: the To field instead of BCC, a file emailed home to finish tonight, a request left unanswered. Nobody makes them on purpose. They make them because they can’t see who’s on the other end.

The core mechanic

Each situation shows a realistic artefact: an email draft, a file, a customer’s letter. Learners pick one of three responses. Beside them, every person involved is a dot inside the company boundary, and the dots react.

What the learner doesWhat happens
Paste 240 subscribers into the To field

→

Send a campaign to a bought list
→
5,000 strangers turn red and the complaints counter climbs to 340.
Make the right call
→
The people stay inside, a lock appears, or the data is deleted for good.

Designing the mistakes

Design decisions

People, not records

The wall shows the real number of people: 1 for an access request, 1,800 for an export, 5,000 for a bought list. Scale is part of the lesson.

Deadlines you can watch

The one-month response time and the 72-hour breach window are clocks on screen, not facts in a slide.

One tempting middle option

Each situation has an answer that sounds responsible but isn’t, like BCC or an unsubscribe link, so learners practise telling “better” from “right”.

No legal jargon

The feedback says what happened and what to do next time, in the words people use at work.
